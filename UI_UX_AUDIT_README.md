## 1. Executive Summary: The Five Biggest Usability Problems

Following a comprehensive audit of the React/TypeScript codebase (`src/App.tsx`, `src/components/views/*`, `src/data/quantModelData.ts`, `src/types/quantTypes.ts`, and `index.html`), the five primary usability and workflow deficiencies are:

1. **Disconnected Decision Flow (The "What Am I Authorizing?" Dilemma):**  
   The application fragments the investment decision across four separate workspaces (`Overview`, `Portfolio`, `Risk`, and `Decision`) without a unified thread. A Chief Investment Officer (CIO) arriving at the Decision view is asked to approve an abstract proposal code (e.g., `PROP-01` or `PROP-02`) in isolation, separated from the before-and-after holdings table, marginal covariance deltas, factor risks, and stress loss projections.

2. **Severe Case-Fact Discrepancies vs. Competition Ground Truth:**  
   The frontend hardcodes an initial capital allocation of **$100,000** and a 5-year liability schedule of **five $10,000 annual commitments ($50,000 nominal total)** starting in 2033 (`LIABILITY_RESERVE_SPEC` in `src/data/quantModelData.ts`). However, the official Wharton competition case rules dictate **$300,000 beginning in 2027, an additional $150,000 beginning in 2028, and ten annual $50,000 operating payments from 2033 through 2042 ($500,000 nominal total)**. Presenting $100k / $50k as verified "Case Facts" exposes the investment committee to decision-making based on contradictory mandate parameters.

3. **Cognitive Overload from Technical & Implementation Jargon:**  
   The interface exposes developer and engineering artifacts directly to the executive user—prominently displaying internal strings such as `Phase 12E Engine`, `DIRECT vs DERIVED classification`, `SHA-256 rawPayloadHash`, `PRIMARY_PROVIDER_PRECEDENCE`, and `SEC_FILING_GROUND_TRUTH`. These system mechanics clutter primary analytical screens rather than being tucked into the progressive audit drawers.

4. **Typography Tri-Family Fragmentation & Excessive Monospace:**  
   The application loads three distinct font families (`Inter`, `Source Serif 4`, and `IBM Plex Mono`). While the recent editorial pass improved visual restraint, `IBM Plex Mono` is overused for non-tabular content, descriptive labels, and table headers. The typography design violates the two-font-family limit and dilutes editorial hierarchy.

5. **Weak Distinctions Between Model Output, Case Facts, and Human Discretion:**  
   Key qualitative assumptions (such as a flat 4.5% risk-free discount rate, a 21.85% geometric compounding rate for 10 years, and a simulated 10% AMZN reallocation) are displayed alongside empirical market data without visual demarcation. The CIO cannot instantly discern what has been observed in real financial markets, what is an extrapolation generated by Python models, and what requires investment committee signoff.

## 3. CIO Comprehension Test

To evaluate the interface from the perspective of an executive decision-maker who did not build the model:

### What the CIO CAN Easily Understand:
* **Current Sleeve Holdings:** The active portfolio holds AAPL (40%), MSFT (35%), NVDA (15%), and Cash (10%), with a heavy concentration in Information Technology (90%).
* **WInS Simulator Compliance Status:** AAPL and MSFT are verified on the Wharton simulator platform, whereas NVDA and AMZN are unverified candidate instruments requiring explicit governance signoff.
* **Deterministic Stress Loss:** The portfolio experiences distinct dollar and percentage losses under specific scenarios (e.g., -39.2% under a 2008 GFC Beta shock, -29.7% under a 2022 Tech rate hike shock).
* **Multi-Provider Discrepancies Exist:** The platform explicitly reveals that Financial Modeling Prep and Alpha Vantage report conflicting values for NVDA's Debt-to-Equity ratio (0.784 vs. 0.766), and that the model selected 0.784 without averaging.

### What the CIO CANNOT Understand or May Misinterpret:
* **The Core Capital Mandate:** The UI claims the client provided $100,000 compounding for 10 years to fund $50,000 in liabilities. If the CIO knows the competition brief specifies $300k in 2027, $150k in 2028, and $500k in operating payments, they will immediately question the quantitative foundation.
* **The Exact Action Being Requested:** In the `Decision` view, the CIO is presented with a dropdown of `PROP-01`, `PROP-02`, `PROP-03`, and `PROP-04`. The interface does not clearly state: *"Are we staying in the Current Sleeve, or are we executing a rebalancing into Proposal 1?"*
* **The Cost/Benefit Tradeoff of Adding AMZN:** While the marginal simulator shows a small volatility decrease (-0.47%) and return change (-0.11%), it does not explain why the analyst chose a 10% weight or which existing holdings are being sold to finance it.
* **The Mathematical Reality of the 10-Year Compounding:** The trajectory chart displays an unbroken exponential curve compounding at 21.85% annually from $100,000 to $721,532. The CIO cannot see any confidence intervals, Monte Carlo dispersion, or probability of failing to meet the reserve.
* **Why NVDA and AMZN are Unverified:** The UI repeats that they are "UNVERIFIED" across five different pages, but never explains *why* (e.g., whether Wharton restricts trading on specific tickers, whether simulator accounts lack penny-stock/tech permissions, or whether real-time data feeds failed to match simulator ticker symbols).

## 6. Typography and Visual Hierarchy Audit

### Current Typography State
* **Declared Font Families:**
  1. `Inter` (sans-serif) — loaded via Google Fonts; used for UI, buttons, body copy.
  2. `Source Serif 4` (serif) — loaded via Google Fonts; used for page headings (`font-serif`).
  3. `IBM Plex Mono` (monospace) — loaded via Google Fonts; used for figures, tables, badges, dates, tickers, and subtitles.
* **Loading Behavior:** All three fonts load via CDN `<link>` in `index.html`. Fallbacks are declared in `src/index.css` (`-apple-system`, `Georgia`, `ui-monospace`).
* **Visual Audit Findings:**
  * **Three Families Cause Visual Disjointedness:** Having a serif title, sans-serif body, and monospace numbers/labels creates a busy layout. The monospace font is applied too broadly—e.g., full table headings, section subtitles, and status notes are rendered in mono, giving the impression of an unfinished terminal.
  * **Excessive Monospace Everywhere:** Monospace is used not just for financial numbers, but for sentences such as `mandateId · 10-Yr Compounding Horizon (Target: Beg-2033)` and `WInS simulator verification boundary`.
  * **Hierarchy Deficiencies:** Section headers (14–16px bold) compete visually with table figures. The eye is drawn to secondary badges rather than key portfolio weights and return deltas.

### Proposed Two-Font-Family Typography Hierarchy
Per project constraints, the typography system must use **no more than two font families across the entire application**. We propose eliminating `Source Serif 4` and establishing a clean, high-contrast, editorial sans-serif hierarchy paired with tabular numerals:

* **Primary Interface & Editorial Typeface:** **Inter** (300 light, 400 regular, 500 medium, 600 semibold).
* **Tabular Figures & Security Identifiers:** **IBM Plex Mono** (400 regular, 500 medium) — **STRICTLY RESERVED** for:
  - Currency values (e.g., `$100,000.00`, `$43,899.80`)
  - Percentages (e.g., `21.85%`, `-0.47%`)
  - Tickers (e.g., `AAPL`, `MSFT`, `SPY`)
  - Precise ratios (e.g., `1.14x`, `0.94`)
  - ISO dates (e.g., `2026-10-05`)
  - Raw SHA-256 hashes

*All headings, labels, table headers, descriptions, navigation buttons, and status explanations must use Inter in normal sentence case.*

## 7. Decision Workflow Audit

The current user journey through the observatory is fragmented. Here is the audit of how a CIO moves from evidence to decision:

```
[Current Fragmented Journey]
Overview (passive glance)
   └─> Research (deep dive into single stock; loses portfolio view)
         └─> Portfolio (looks at AMZN marginal slider or Proposal 1-4 cards)
               └─> Risk (looks at 5 stress bars and trajectory)
                     └─> Decision (presented with dropdown: PROP-01, PROP-02... must remember metrics)
```

### Critical Gaps in the Decision Flow:
1. **Lack of a Unified Recommendation:**  
   The application does not present a clear analyst thesis: *"We recommend shifting from Current Sleeve to Proposal 2 (Unconstrained 4-Asset) because AMZN reduces IT sector risk and adds +0.018 Sharpe, but it requires a CIO waiver for NVDA and AMZN."*
2. **Context Amnesia at Decision Time:**  
   When the CIO switches to the `Decision` workspace, the stress scenario losses, marginal deltas, and security fundamentals are hidden behind previous tabs. The CIO must flip back and forth between tabs to confirm the numbers.
3. **WInS Governance Friction:**  
   The CIO is presented with a red warning note about unverified securities, but the action required is ambiguous. Is the CIO signing off on a trade, or granting a competition compliance exception?

### Recommended Integrated CIO Decision Sequence:
We propose structuring the CIO workflow into an intuitive 5-step progression anchored in the Decision workspace:

```
[Recommended Cohesive Journey]
Step 1: Mandate & Active Portfolio Baseline
        (Review current weights: AAPL 40%, MSFT 35%, NVDA 15%, Cash 10%)
Step 2: Recommended Reallocation & Marginal Fit
        (Inspect Proposed Sleeve vs. Current Sleeve delta table side-by-side)
Step 3: Risk & Stress Impact
        (Review changes in Volatility, Beta, and 2008/2022 stress test drawdowns)
Step 4: Governance & WInS Compliance Attestation
        (Review specific exceptions: NVDA 15% and AMZN 10% unverified waivers)
Step 5: Executive Signoff & Memorandum Archive
        (Record decision state: Approved, Approved with Conditions, or Rejected)
```

## 8. Financial Integrity and Source-Traceability Issues

During the quantitative code audit of `src/data/quantModelData.ts` and `src/components/views/*`, several critical data discrepancies and traceability issues were flagged:

### 1. Mandate Capital Injection & Liability Payment Discrepancy
* **Frontend Implementation (`src/data/quantModelData.ts`, lines 25–27, 1024–1035):**  
  Hardcodes initial capital as `$100,000` in 2023, zero future capital contributions, and a 5-year post-2033 liability schedule of `$10,000 / year` ($50,000 total nominal; PV = `$43,899.80`).
* **Official Wharton Case Brief:**  
  The actual Wharton High School Investment Competition case parameters dictate:
  - **Capital Contributions:** $300,000 contribution beginning in 2027, followed by an additional $150,000 beginning in 2028.
  - **Operating Liabilities:** Ten annual operating payments of $50,000 each from 2033 through 2042 ($500,000 total nominal liabilities).
* **Audit Finding:** **SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED (P0).** The frontend displays model calculations based on a simplified $100k principal / $10k liability schedule that conflicts with the competition's actual cash-flow rules. The trajectory curve of $721,532 at Beginning-2033 assumes zero intermediate capital contributions and fails to model the 2027 ($300k) and 2028 ($150k) inflows.

### 2. Frontend-Simulated Marginal Sliders
* **Frontend Implementation (`src/components/views/PortfolioView.tsx`, lines 39–58):**  
  The marginal fit simulation for AMZN at 5%, 10%, and 15% contains frontend math approximations:
  ```typescript
  const w = fitWeight / 100;
  const ret = 0.2185 * (1 - w) + 0.208 * w;
  const vol = 0.1842 - 0.0047 * (fitWeight / 10);
  const sharpe = (ret - 0.045) / vol;
  const beta = 1.14 * (1 - w) + 1.22 * w;
  ```
* **Audit Finding:** **CALCULATION ISOLATION VIOLATION (P0).** The original Python model contains exact covariance matrices for the 4-asset universe. Hardcoding linear approximations (`- 0.0047 * (fitWeight / 10)`) directly inside a React `useMemo` hook violates the constraint against inventing calculations in the frontend.

### 3. Flat 4.5% Risk-Free Discount Rate Assumption
* **Frontend Implementation (`src/data/quantModelData.ts`, line 28; `src/components/views/RiskView.tsx`, line 28):**  
  Uses a fixed 4.5% discount rate to calculate the present value of liabilities at Beginning-2033.
* **Audit Finding:** **MODEL ASSUMPTION DISCLOSURE REQUIRED (P1).** A 4.5% discount rate over a 10-to-15 year horizon is a significant economic assumption. It must be clearly flagged as a *Model Assumption*, not a fixed *Case Fact*.

### 4. Deterministic Stress vs. Historical Forecast Confusion
* **Frontend Implementation (`src/data/quantModelData.ts`, lines 935–1008):**  
  Scenarios such as `Scenario 3: 2008 GFC Market Beta Shock (-38.0% S&P 500)` and `Scenario 4: 2022 Tech Valuation & Rates Drawdown (-33.0% Tech)` apply static beta multipliers.
* **Audit Finding:** **EPISTEMIC LABELING REQUIRED (P1).** The UI must explicitly remind the CIO that these are deterministic conditional sensitivity stress tests, not historical backtests or predictive forward forecasts.

## 10. Proposed Final Navigation

Rather than five competing tabs with overlapping data, the recommended navigation structure provides a streamlined four-step analytical workflow plus an audit utility:

```
[Top Navigation Bar]
1. Executive Briefing (Overview & Mandate Status)
2. Security Research (Dossier, GAAP Fundamentals & Screening)
3. Portfolio Analytics (Current Allocations & Construction Comparison)
4. CIO Decision & Risk (Proposal Selection, Delta Matrix, Stress Impact & Signoff)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Utility Right]
```

### Justification for Consolidating Risk into CIO Decision:

Integrating deterministic stress impacts directly into the Decision workspace eliminates context-switching and creates an authentic executive dashboard.
